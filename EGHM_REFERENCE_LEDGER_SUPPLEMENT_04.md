# EGHM reference ledger — Supplement 04: peat biogeomorphology, Korean montane wetlands, and source-verification corrections

Updated: 2026-08-27

## Scope

This supplement strengthens the evidence chain linking vegetation, peat accumulation, wetland surface development, and terrestrialization. It also audits several references already used in earlier Seoyeongari theses/reports and distinguishes what those sources actually support from claims that were too strong.

---

# M1. Temmink et al. (2022) — wetland biogeomorphic feedbacks

Temmink, R.J.M., Lamers, L.P.M., Angelini, C., Bouma, T.J., Fritz, C., van de Koppel, J., Lexmond, R., Rietkerk, M., Silliman, B.R., Joosten, H. & van der Heide, T. (2022). **Recovering wetland biogeomorphic feedbacks to restore the world's biotic carbon hotspots.** *Science*, 376(6593), eabn1479. DOI: **10.1126/science.abn1479**.

Evidence class: **DIRECT BIOGEOMORPHIC-FEEDBACK REVIEW / SYNTHESIS**.

Key support:
- major wetland carbon sinks are maintained by feedbacks between landscape-building organisms and geomorphic/environmental conditions;
- vegetation can modify sediment/organic-matter accumulation and physical habitat, while the resulting landscape state feeds back on vegetation and hydrology;
- degradation can disrupt these positive biogeomorphic feedbacks, while restoration may require recovering them rather than manipulating one process in isolation.

Use in EGHM:
- one of the strongest high-level references for treating peat accumulation and vegetation development as a **biogeomorphic process**, not merely as independent empirical corrections;
- supports the conceptual rationale for an Integrated hydro–ecology–geomorphology scenario.

Does **not** determine:
- Seoyeongari peat accumulation rate;
- the EGHM observation-operator coefficients;
- the exact form of `G`, `Kc`, or any hydrologic reservoir coefficient.

# M2. Yu, Kim & Kim (2022) — Korean montane wetlands

Yu, H.Y., Kim, S.H. & Kim, J.G. (2022). **Carbon sequestration potential in montane wetlands of Korea.** *Global Ecology and Conservation*, 37, e02166. DOI: **10.1016/j.gecco.2022.e02166**.

Evidence class: **KOREAN MONTANE-WETLAND ANALOGUE / PEAT-CARBON FIELD EVIDENCE**.

Key support:
- quantifies carbon storage and sequestration in montane fen wetlands in Odaesan National Park, Korea;
- demonstrates that organic-layer depth varies among montane wetlands and is important for estimating carbon storage per unit area;
- emphasizes that dominant vegetation is relevant to carbon sequestration and that Sphagnum can affect the balance by reducing carbon emissions;
- provides a Korean montane-wetland context in which peat/organic-layer development, vegetation composition and carbon accumulation are spatially heterogeneous.

Use in EGHM:
- strong Korean analogue showing that vegetation and organic-layer depth must be considered jointly when interpreting peat accumulation/carbon sequestration;
- useful external comparison for the Seoyeongari field-derived peat and carbon measurements.

Important limitation:
- Odaesan accumulation/carbon rates are **comparison values**, not parameters to transfer into Seoyeongari;
- differences in dominant vegetation, hydrology, climate, peat age and disturbance prevent direct rate transfer.

# M3. Loisel et al. (2017) — peat basin geometry and age-model uncertainty

Loisel, J., van Bellen, S., Pelletier, L., Talbot, J., Hugelius, G., Karran, D., Yu, Z., Nichols, J. & Holmquist, J. (2017). **Insights and issues with estimating northern peatland carbon stocks and fluxes since the Last Glacial Maximum.** *Earth-Science Reviews*, 165, 59–80. DOI: **10.1016/j.earscirev.2016.12.001**.

Evidence class: **PEAT-CARBON METHOD REVIEW / SPATIAL & CHRONOLOGICAL CAVEAT**.

Key support:
- explicitly identifies **peat-basin heterogeneity** as an important source of uncertainty in estimating peat volume and carbon stocks at local scale;
- notes that palaeoecological cores are often taken near peatland centres or thickest deposits, so simple extrapolation without representing peat-mass geometry can bias whole-site estimates;
- identifies age-model selection and chronological control as major uncertainties when reconstructing carbon accumulation rates from peat cores;
- emphasizes vegetation assemblage differences when estimating peatland carbon stocks/fluxes.

Use in Seoyeongari:
- strongly supports measuring/mapping peat depth at multiple locations rather than extrapolating the deepest/core-centre profile uniformly over the wetland;
- supports explicit caution around the Clymo accumulation estimate because radiocarbon control is sparse;
- supports retaining lower/central/upper long-term peat estimates rather than pretending that the central estimate has negligible uncertainty.

### Correction to a previous Seoyeongari report statement

The field report states that using a deepest single core can overestimate total storage by "about 30%" and cites Loisel et al. (2017). The Loisel review clearly supports the **direction and importance of peat-basin geometry bias**, but this audit has not yet located a universal 30% figure in the Loisel review itself.

Therefore manuscript-safe wording is:

> Ignoring within-wetland peat-depth/basin geometry can bias peat-volume and carbon-stock estimates (Loisel et al., 2017).

Do **not** attribute an exact universal `30%` overestimate to Loisel et al. (2017) unless the specific underlying calculation/source is located.

A later empirical study (peat bathymetry vs. non-bathymetric estimates) reported mean overestimates of roughly 20–38% depending on method, reinforcing that the magnitude can indeed be of this order but is site/method dependent.

# M4. Leith et al. (2014) — source verification and correction

Leith, F.I., Garnett, M.H., Dinsmore, K.J., Billett, M.F. & Heal, K.V. (2014). **Source and age of dissolved and gaseous carbon in a peatland–riparian–stream continuum: a dual isotope (14C and δ13C) analysis.** *Biogeochemistry*, 119(1–3), 415–433. DOI: **10.1007/s10533-014-9977-y**.

Evidence class: **PEAT CARBON AGE / PROFILE & AQUATIC-CONNECTIVITY STUDY**.

What the paper actually supports:
- Auchencorth Moss is an ombrotrophic peatland with peat deposits ranging from **<0.5 m to 5 m thick** across the catchment;
- peat soil radiocarbon age increases with depth;
- carbon pools and decomposition products differ between peatland, riparian zone and stream, and modern carbon can be transported vertically through the soil profile;
- peatland–riparian–stream carbon connectivity is hydrologically meaningful.

### Correction to old Seoyeongari thesis wording

An earlier Seoyeongari undergraduate thesis stated that Leith et al. (2014) showed Auchencorth Moss was composed of an O horizon down to 150 cm. This exact claim is **not supported by the verified Leith paper**. The verified site description instead reports peat thickness varying from <0.5 to 5 m.

Action:
- **DO NOT reuse** the "150 cm O-layer" statement with Leith et al. (2014) as its citation;
- Leith et al. may still be used for peat-profile carbon age/connectivity context if relevant, but it is not necessary to establish that Seoyeongari itself is peat-rich because Seoyeongari has direct soil-core evidence.

This correction illustrates the ledger rule: a bibliographic citation is insufficient unless the source actually supports the claimed fact.

# M5. Seoyeongari undergraduate thesis — site-primary biogeomorphic evidence

Woo, S.J. **Analysis of Biogeomorphic Processes and Carbon Storage Capacities of Seoyeongari Wetland in Jeju Island** [제주도 서영아리 습지의 생물지형학적 프로세스 및 탄소보유능 분석]. Seoul National University Department of Geography undergraduate thesis/project manuscript.

Evidence class: **SITE PRIMARY**.

Verified site observations contained in the archived manuscript:
- mean organic-matter content is very high and broadly persistent with soil depth (reported approximately 0.50–0.65 in sampled peat profiles);
- vegetation shows a spatial gradient from central graminoid/sedge wetland vegetation through *Ludwigia* / transitional vegetation toward terrestrial vegetation;
- a transitional ecotone is observed around the wetland margin;
- historical imagery indicates a long-term contraction of the mapped open-water/wetland pond footprint.

Use in EGHM:
- direct site support that the wetland contains substantial organic soil and a vegetation transition consistent with terrestrialization;
- direct site motivation for testing a hydrosere/vegetation-encroachment mechanism and a peat-related biogeomorphic mechanism together.

Critical current-data rule:
- the old thesis includes a 2022 mapped pond area, but the **current EGHM analysis explicitly treats 2022 pond area as absent** by user decision. The old thesis value must not be reintroduced into current fitting, validation, scoring or scenario selection.

# M6. Seoyeongari carbon-cycle field report — site-primary peat evidence

Woo, S.J. & Kim, J.N. **Analysis of carbon cycling in a Jeju peat wetland** [제주도 이탄습지의 탄소순환 분석]. Archived student autonomous-education final report.

Evidence class: **SITE PRIMARY**.

Relevant evidence already locked in the current model audit:
- multiple peat-depth measurements were used to avoid assuming spatially uniform peat thickness;
- Core 13 radiocarbon control at 75 cm was used with a Clymo-model formulation to derive long-term peat accumulation lower/central/upper estimates of **0.29 / 0.38 / 0.47 mm yr-1**;
- recent apparent accumulation estimates are much higher (**2.89 / 5.91 / 7.00 mm yr-1**) and are not propagated as sustained long-term topographic rise in the EGHM;
- the report itself acknowledges limitations due to sparse radiocarbon dating.

Use in EGHM:
- direct source for the central peat-rate input **0.38 mm yr-1**;
- direct reason to retain 0.29 and 0.47 as site-informed lower/upper sensitivity values;
- direct reason to keep recent apparent rates as stress-test context rather than central geomorphic parameters.

# M7. Evidence hierarchy for the peat component

For final manuscript claims, use evidence in this order:

1. **Seoyeongari direct measurements/report** — site peat depth, OM, radiocarbon-derived accumulation estimate;
2. **Korean montane-wetland literature (Yu et al., 2022)** — regional ecological/carbon context;
3. **process syntheses (Temmink et al., 2022; Morris et al., 2011; Waddington et al., 2015)** — justification for coupling ecology, hydrology and peat/geomorphology;
4. **peat methodological reviews (Loisel et al., 2017; Young et al., 2019; Cahoon, 2024)** — uncertainty and interpretation limits;
5. international site analogues only where needed.

This hierarchy prevents an external analogue from overriding direct Seoyeongari evidence.

# M8. Manuscript-safe claims now supported

### Strongly supported

> Seoyeongari contains a substantial organic/peat soil body and exhibits a wetland-to-terrestrial vegetation transition around the pond margin.

> Wetland vegetation, hydrology and organic-matter/landscape-building processes can interact through biogeomorphic feedbacks.

> Spatial peat-depth heterogeneity should be represented or sampled rather than assuming one deepest core applies uniformly across a wetland.

> The central long-term peat accumulation parameter of 0.38 mm yr-1 is independently site-derived rather than selected by fitting pond-area observations.

### Supported with explicit caveat

> Long-term preserved peat accumulation is used in EGHM as a persistent biogeomorphic surface-rise proxy.

Caveat: accumulation is not identical to directly measured surface-elevation change because compaction, decomposition, root-zone expansion/contraction and shallow subsidence may contribute.

### Not supported / do not claim

> Leith et al. (2014) demonstrated a 150-cm O horizon at Auchencorth Moss.

> Loisel et al. (2017) established a universal 30% single-core overestimate.

> External peatland studies determine the Seoyeongari 0.38 mm yr-1 rate.

> The literature proves that the Integrated model must rank first.

# M9. Consequence for further model development

These references strengthen the current peat/biogeomorphic **process structure** and reduce the rationale for adding another peat-growth coefficient. Remaining improvement priorities are therefore empirical/diagnostic rather than structural:

1. preserve 0.38 mm yr-1 as the site-derived Clymo central estimate;
2. keep 0.29–0.47 mm yr-1 as the site-derived long-term uncertainty/sensitivity envelope;
3. do not equate this with direct surface-elevation measurements;
4. retain spatial peat heterogeneity in any carbon-stock discussion;
5. focus additional model robustness work on the unresolved inherited hydrology constants rather than adding new peat parameters.
